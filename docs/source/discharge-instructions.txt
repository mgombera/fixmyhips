                                  Hip Arthroscopy
                         Post-Operative Instruction Checklist

                FOLLOW-UP APPOINTMENT: _______________________________

DRESSING
   • Maintain your operative dressing. You may remove the outer bulky dressing 48 hours after surgery.
   • It is normal for the hip to bleed and swell or become bruised following surgery.
   • If blood soaks onto the bandage, do not become alarmed – reinforce with additional dressing.
   • Please keep steri-strips (butterfly bandages) in place until they fall off on their own.
   • If dressing supplies were sent, remove the steri-strips and apply the dressings on post op day 4
   • Do not put any ointments or creams over the incisions.

BATHING
   • Keep surgical incisions clean and dry.
   • You may shower 48 hours after surgery. Place waterproof bandaids or plastic over the incision areas.
      Please remember to change bandaids daily.
   • NO immersion of operative leg (i.e. bath)
   • Brace may come off to shower.

ACTIVITY
   • Elevate the operative leg to chest level whenever possible to decrease swelling.
   • You will be partial weight bearing (20#) with crutches after surgery.
   • Use the brace when walking and sitting. It is not needed at night.
   • Do not engage in activities which increase hip pain (prolonged periods of standing or walking).
   • Avoid long periods of sitting or long distance traveling for 2 weeks after surgery.
   • You may return to sedentary work or school 4-7 days after surgery, if pain is tolerable.

BRACE
   • Your brace should be worn when walking for the first 4 weeks after surgery.
   • The brace can be removed to shower, during exercises, and at night.

ICE THERAPY
   • Icing is important in the initial post-operative period and should begin immediately after surgery.
   • Use ice machine continuously or ice packs (if machine not purchased) for 30-45 minutes every 2
      hours daily until your first post-operative visit.
   • Care should be taken with icing to avoid frostbite to the skin.
   • You do not need to wake up in the middle of the night to change over the ice machine or icepacks
      unless you are uncomfortable.

DRIVING
   • Please do not drive while you are taking narcotic pain medication.
   • It is best to wait to start driving until you are evaluated in the office after surgery.

EXERCISES
   • Discomfort and hip stiffness is normal for a few days following surgery. It is safe to bend the hip
     within the brace parameters. Avoid flexing the hip past 90 degrees.
   • Perform ankle pumps continuously throughout the day.
   • Formal physical therapy (PT) typically begins 3-5 days after surgery. A prescription and protocol
     are provided with your discharge paperwork.

MEDICATIONS
  • It is not uncommon for patients to encounter more pain on the first or second day after surgery when
     swelling peaks. Use your pain medication as directed.
  • Common side effects of the pain medication are nausea, drowsiness, and constipation. To decrease
     the side effects, take the medication with food. If constipation occurs, consider taking an over the
     counter laxative and be sure to drink plenty of water.
  • If you are having problems with nausea and vomiting, contact the office.
  • Do not drive a car or operate machinery while taking the narcotic medication.
  • Please avoid alcohol use while taking narcotic pain medication.
  • For 2 weeks following surgery take one Aspirin 81mg tablet twice daily to lower the risk of
     developing a blood clot after surgery.

FOLLOW-UP VISIT
   • If you need to reschedule your follow-up appointment, please call us at 713-794-3457.
   • Typically, the first post-operative appointment following surgery is 10-14 days following surgery.
   • The first post-operative appointment will be with our Physician Assistant. She will assess the
     incision, go over the surgery and your recovery, and answer any questions you may have.

EMERGENCIES
  • Contact us if any of the following are present:
       o Painful swelling or numbness (note that some swelling and numbness is normal)
       o Unrelenting pain
       o Fever (over 101° – it is normal to have a low grade following surgery) or chills
       o Continuous drainage or bleeding from incision (a small amount of drainage is expected)
       o Difficulty breathing
       o Excessive nausea/vomiting
       o Calf pain
